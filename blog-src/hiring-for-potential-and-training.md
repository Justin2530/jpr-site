Most job descriptions start as a wish list. Years of experience, specific equipment or software, certifications, industry background. Then the search begins, and the person who checks every box never turns up.

In a smaller labor market like Jefferson County and the surrounding counties, that's common. The exact match may not exist nearby, or they may already have a job they're happy with. Meanwhile, the position sits open.

## Separate the must-haves from the nice-to-haves

Before the search goes any further, look hard at the requirements and sort them into two groups:

- **Must-haves:** what the person truly needs on day one, such as a license the job legally requires, or the ability to do the physical work safely.
- **Things you can teach:** your specific equipment, your software, your processes and the way your shop or office does things.

Many requirements that feel essential are really in the second group. A good employee picks up "how we do it here" no matter where they came from.

## What's harder to teach

Skills can be learned. Some things are much harder to train:

- **Reliability.** Showing up on time, every day, ready to work.
- **Attitude.** Willingness to learn, take direction and help the people around them.
- **Mechanical or technical aptitude.** Someone who has already shown they can figure things out usually picks up the next thing quickly.
- **Fit with the schedule and pay.** If the hours and pay don't work for them, training won't fix that.

Someone strong in these areas with some of the skills is often a better hire than someone with every skill who doesn't show up.

## Make training part of the plan

If you decide to hire for potential, plan for it:

- **Decide who will train them** and give that person the time to do it.
- **Write down the first few weeks**, even roughly, so the new hire knows what to expect.
- **Check in often early on.** Small questions answered quickly keep a new hire from getting frustrated and leaving.
- **Say it in the job posting.** "We'll train the right person" brings in people who would otherwise assume they aren't qualified.
